The room

Desks, a break area and a door: what is in the office, and what the people in it do there.

Rooms and floors

Every player has a room. The player sits at the top in the middle, at a desk of their own, and their agents sit two on each side of an aisle below them. A room holds four agents; a fifth opens the player's next room, joined to the first by a door.

A floor holds up to three players and ten agents each. Agents beyond that go to the next floor, which looks the same. Each agent keeps the desk they were given, and a desk an agent leaves goes to the next one to arrive. At most one free desk shows, faded and marked Standby.

No two rooms are furnished quite alike: each has its own plants, cabinets, boxes, pictures and rug, and the same room always looks the same.

Your character

When you first come into a multiplayer office, you choose your character. You sit at your desk even with no agents of your own, and clicking yourself is how you choose again.

At the desk

Each desk has a computer, and each agent keeps its own program on the screen: code, a chart, a dashboard, a chat, a terminal or a landscape. People at work type, lean in to the screen, glance around, look up to think, sip their coffee, scratch their head and stretch, each at their own pace, so a room never moves in step. Somebody with nothing to do sits back with the screen off now and then.

The break area

Every floor shares one: a cabinet with the coffee machine and mugs, a fridge, a microwave on a little table, a water cooler, a noticeboard, a table with chairs and a plant.

Between jobs, agents get up now and then: for a coffee, a glass of water or something from the microwave, or just to stretch their legs. Two who meet there may chat for a while. Only two are away at a time, and anyone who gets real work while they are up goes straight back. None of it changes their work or what the panels say.

The break area also has the office's front door. A new agent comes in through it, gets a coffee and takes it to their desk. Each agent does that once, the first time you see them.

Windows, the clock and the light

The windows and clocks follow the computer the office is hosted on, not yours: its time of day, from dawn to night, and its weather, as rain, snow, fog, cloud or a storm across the glass. The light in the room changes with the time of day too. Everyone watching sees the same.

The bookshelf

Each room has a bookshelf. Click it to open the office's shared files, where you can share a file with the agents and the other players.

Statuses

The Agents panel lists everyone with their status, in four families: Idle (idle, resting, drinking), Working (working, researching, waiting), Delivering (delivering, error) and Moving (walking, dancing). The family sets the colour, and each status within it is a shade of that colour.

Subagents

When one agent fans out into several, the children are not drawn: a fan-out looks like one busy person.

What the characters are called is agent names.
